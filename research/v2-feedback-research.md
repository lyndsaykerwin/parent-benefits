# V2 feedback research: fact checks for Bundle

Researched Oct 25, 2026. Every claim has a source link; "checked" dates are Oct 25, 2026 unless noted. Where the only source is a law firm, benefits company or news site (not a government site), that is said plainly.

Short glossary (words used below):
- **Dependent Care FSA (DCFSA):** a work benefit that lets you pay for daycare with money taken from your paycheck before taxes.
- **Tax credit:** money taken straight off your tax bill. "Refundable" means you can get it back as a refund even if you owe no tax.
- **AGI (adjusted gross income):** your income for tax purposes, after a few set deductions.
- **Special enrollment / qualifying life event:** a short window outside the normal yearly sign-up period when a big life change (like a birth) lets you add people to health insurance.
- **Grandfathered plan:** an old health plan that has not changed much since March 2010, so it does not have to follow some newer health-law rules.
- **Self-funded (self-insured) plan:** the employer pays medical bills itself and hires an insurance company only to run the paperwork. Many big employers do this.
- **ERISA:** the federal law that governs most job-based benefit plans.
- **API:** a door that lets one computer program ask another for data in a tidy, machine-readable way.
- **Scraping:** having a program read web pages built for humans and pull data out. Sites often block it or forbid it in their rules.

## 1. Dependent Care FSA (2026) and the Child and Dependent Care Credit

**Bottom line:** Yes, $7,500 is correct for 2026. It is a per-household cap ($3,750 each if married filing separately). A couple where one parent stays home and earns nothing generally cannot use it, unless that parent is a full-time student, disabled, or actively looking for work. Self-employed people cannot join a DCFSA through their own business in the usual way; they use the tax credit instead, which got bigger in 2026 (up to 50% of $3,000/$6,000 of costs). A single working parent qualifies.

**Facts**
- The 2025 tax law (P.L. 119-21, section 70404, which the IRS calls the "Working Families Tax Cuts") raised the DCFSA tax-free limit from $5,000 to **$7,500 starting in 2026**, or from $2,500 to **$3,750 for married people filing separately**. Source: IRS, "Child and Dependent Care Credit information," last reviewed Oct 15, 2026, https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-and-dependent-care-credit-information ; IRS correction notice for the 2026 W-2 instructions, https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/correction-to-the-dependent-care-benefits-exclusion-amount-in-the-2026-general-instructions-for-forms-w-2-and-w-3
- It is a household limit, not per parent: the law caps a married employee at the lesser of their own or their spouse's earned income, and the overall cap applies to the joint return. Source: 26 U.S. Code §129(b), https://www.customsmobile.com/uscode/title26_subtitleA_chapter1_subchapterB_partIII_section129 (copy of the U.S. Code, updated Jul 28, 2026).
- **Employers do not have to raise their plan to $7,500.** Some plans may still cap at $5,000. Source: Polsinelli (law firm), https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/new-year-new-dependent-care-fsa-limits ; Hill Ward Henderson (law firm), https://www.hwhlaw.com/advisory-dependent-care-fsa-limit-increased-to-7-500-for-2026
- **Stay-at-home parent:** Because the limit is "the lesser of" the two spouses' earned income, a spouse with $0 earnings makes the limit $0. Exceptions: a spouse who is a full-time student or unable to care for themselves is treated as earning $250 a month (one child) or $500 a month (two or more). Sources: §129(b) above; IRS Publication 503 (2025), https://www.irs.gov/publications/p503 . Benefits firm Newfront adds the "actively looking for work" case: https://newfront.com/blog/dependent-care-fsa-issues-for-married-couples
- A spouse who works from home or is self-employed does have earned income, so the household can still qualify. Source: Newfront, https://newfront.com/blog/dependent-care-fsa-where-spouse-works-from-home-or-is-self-employed
- **Self-employed people:** The law that allows these pre-tax plans (Section 125 "cafeteria plans") only lets *employees* join. Sole owners, partners, LLC members and owners of more than 2% of an S-corporation do not count. Sources: HUB International compliance bulletin, Apr 2026, https://www.hubinternational.com/products/employee-benefits/compliance-bulletins/2026/04/section-125-plans-and-self-employed-rules-and-limits/ ; NFP, https://www.nfp.com/insights/owner-employees-cafeteria-plan-eligibility-explained/ . (A business owner can set up a separate "dependent care assistance program," but that needs an accountant. For the app, the simple route is the credit.)
- **The credit (Child and Dependent Care Credit), 2026 rules:** up to **50%** of costs (was 35%), on up to **$3,000 of costs for one child / $6,000 for two or more**, so a credit of up to **$1,500 / $3,000**. The 50% rate applies at AGI of $15,000 or less, slides to 35%, then drops again above $75,000 ($150,000 married filing jointly), never below 20%. It is **not refundable**. It applies to 2026 tax returns filed in 2027. Source: IRS page above (Oct 15, 2026).
- **Can't double-dip:** costs paid with the DCFSA cannot also be counted for the credit. If you put the full $7,500 in a DCFSA, there's no credit left, since $7,500 is more than the $6,000 cap. Source: same IRS page.
- **Self-employment income counts** as earned income for the credit. Source: IRS Pub 503, https://www.irs.gov/publications/p503
- **Single working parent:** qualifies for both; the limit is just their own earned income (up to $7,500). Source: §129(b).
- **Deadline note for the app:** the app mockup says the DCFSA has "30 days from the birth to sign up." No federal rule sets that number. A birth lets you change a DCFSA election mid-year, but each employer's plan sets the window (often 30 or 31 days). Say "usually 30 days; check with HR." (Not separately verified today: the mid-year change rules are in Treasury Regulation 1.125-4, and the 30-day HIPAA minimum in section 2 applies to health plans, not DCFSAs.)

## 2. Adding a newborn to health insurance

**Bottom line:** Job-based plan: at least 30 days by federal law (some plans give more), with coverage back-dated to the birth. Marketplace (in Massachusetts, the Health Connector): 60 days, back-dated to the birth. Medicaid/CHIP (MassHealth): a baby born to a mom on MassHealth is covered automatically for the first year, no application; other families can apply any time. TRICARE: register the baby in DEERS within 90 days (120 overseas). The app's "30 days" is right for job-based plans only.

**Facts**
- **Job-based plans (HIPAA special enrollment):** plans "must allow ... at least 30 days" to request enrollment after a birth, and coverage must start on the date of birth. Sources: U.S. Department of Labor, https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/EBSA/health/employer/1C18.asp?1C17=N (citing 29 CFR 2590.701-6(b)(3)); DOL HIPAA FAQ, https://www.dol.gov/node/25144 . Some plans give 31 or 60 days; that is a plan choice, not the law. Example plan notice: Northwestern University, https://hr.northwestern.edu/documents/benefits/hipaa_specialenrollment.pdf
- **Marketplace (HealthCare.gov and state versions):** apply within 60 days after the birth; coverage can start on the day of the birth. Source: HealthCare.gov, https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/ and https://www.healthcare.gov/what-if-im-pregnant-or-plan-to-get-pregnant/
- **Medicaid/CHIP "deemed newborns":** a baby whose mother was on Medicaid (or CHIP as a pregnant woman) on the birth date is covered from birth to the first birthday **without an application**, regardless of changes in circumstances. Sources: 42 CFR 435.117 (eCFR), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-435/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRe8b37da39612951/section-435.117 ; CHIP version 42 CFR 457.360, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/457.360 ; CMS guidance, https://www.medicaid.gov/federal-policy-guidance/downloads/SHO083109b.pdf . Medicaid/CHIP enrollment is open year-round: https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage-outside-open-enrollment/special-enrollment-period/
- **TRICARE:** register the baby in DEERS (the military's family enrollment system) within **90 days in the U.S. or 120 days overseas**. Active duty: the baby is then placed in a plan automatically. Retirees: must also enroll the baby within 90 days (120 overseas) or claims are denied starting day 91 (121). The birth also opens a 90-day window for the whole family to change plans. Sources: TRICARE, https://tricare.mil/LifeEvents/Baby ; TRICARE Newsroom, https://newsroom.tricare.mil/News/TRICARE-News/Article/4407472/how-to-enroll-your-newborn-in-tricare ; TRICARE FAQ, https://tricare.mil/FAQs/General/GEN_New_Child_Coverage
- **Massachusetts specifics:**
  - Health Connector birth enrollment can be back-dated to the birth date (most other special enrollments only start going forward). Source: Massachusetts Legal Services, https://masslegalservices.org/node/153872
  - A baby born to a mother on MassHealth Standard or Limited is automatically eligible for MassHealth Standard for one year. Families must report the birth to MassHealth **within 10 days**; if the mother is not on MassHealth, newborn coverage is back-dated only 10 days from the application. The hospital usually files a "Notification of Birth" (NOB-1) form. Sources: MassHealth provider bulletin 305, https://www.mass.gov/doc/all-provider-bulletin-305-policies-and-procedure-for-newborn-members-eligibility-enrollment-and-payment/download ; Mass Legal Services above. (These MassHealth documents are from about 2021-2022; worth a quick re-check with MassHealth before the demo.)

## 3. What every non-grandfathered plan covers at $0, and is carrier name worth asking?

**Bottom line:** The federal $0 list (breast pump and lactation help, well-baby visits, newborn screenings, key prenatal screenings) is the same for every carrier: Aetna, BCBS, UHC, Kaiser, and also self-funded employer plans. The real exceptions are grandfathered plans and short-term plans. The Supreme Court upheld this in June 2025. Carriers do add extras (free maternity coaching apps, often Maven; their own list of pump suppliers), but those depend on the employer's contract more than on the carrier's brand. **Asking for the carrier name is worth it only as an optional question, to show the right "order your pump here" link. The more useful question is "Where does your insurance come from: work, Marketplace/Health Connector, MassHealth, TRICARE?"**

**Facts**
- **Breastfeeding:** plans must cover lactation support, counseling and equipment for as long as you breastfeed, before and after birth; a pump can be rented or kept. Grandfathered plans are excluded. Source: HealthCare.gov, https://www.healthcare.gov/coverage/breast-feeding-benefits/ . HRSA's guideline (updated 2022, for plans starting in 2023) names **double electric pumps and milk storage supplies**. Source: HRSA Women's Preventive Services Guidelines, https://www.hrsa.gov/es/node/364
- **Children:** well-child visits, immunizations, newborn screenings (hearing, bilirubin, sickle cell, thyroid, PKU), developmental and autism screenings, and more, free from in-network providers. Source: HealthCare.gov, https://www.healthcare.gov/preventive-care-children/ . (This list follows the American Academy of Pediatrics' "Bright Futures" schedule; HealthCare.gov does not name it on that page.)
- **Self-funded employer plans count too.** The rule applies to all non-grandfathered plans, fully insured and self-insured, including ERISA plans. Source: U.S. Senate letter summarizing the rule, Sept 25, 2024, https://www.duckworth.senate.gov/news/press-releases/duckworth-merkley-push-to-ensure-health-insurers-cover-and-provide-lactation-care-for-pregnant-and-nursing-parents ; CMS ACA FAQ Set 12 (if no in-network provider exists, the plan must cover out-of-network at $0), https://www.cms.gov/CCIIO/Resources/Fact-Sheets-and-FAQs/aca_implementation_faqs12
- **Grandfathered plans** do not have to follow these rules; self-funded plans can also be grandfathered. Source: Horton Group, https://www.thehortongroup.com/resources/compliance-checklist-for-determining-grandfathered-status
- **Short-term plans** are exempt from ACA consumer protections. Source: CMS statement on short-term plans, https://www.cms.gov/files/document/statement-regarding-short-term-limited-duration-insurance.pdf ; a rule to lengthen short-term plans was expected summer 2026: Bloomberg Tax, https://news.bloombergtax.com/health-law-and-business/agencies-target-summer-2026-for-short-term-health-plans-rule
- **Braidwood (Kennedy v. Braidwood Management), decided June 27, 2025:** the Supreme Court ruled 6-3 that the free-preventive-care requirement tied to the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force is constitutional. The status quo stayed. Sources: KFF, https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/kennedy-v-braidwood-the-supreme-court-upheld-aca-preventive-services-but-thats-not-the-end-of-the-story/ ; AAMC, https://www.aamc.org/advocacy-policy/washington-highlights/supreme-court-issues-braidwood-opinion-preserves-preventive-care-requirement . A related question about vaccine recommendations (ACIP) went back to the lower court. Source: Health Affairs, https://www.healthaffairs.org/content/forefront/insurance-plan-coverage-policies-help-clarify-acip-confusion-probably-not
- **Live caution for babies (vaccines):** the January 2026 federal childhood vaccine schedule moved the hepatitis B birth dose from "everyone" to "talk with your doctor." A federal court in Massachusetts paused that schedule on March 16, 2026; the government appealed on April 29, 2026. Health insurers (through their trade group AHIP) pledged to keep covering vaccines that were recommended as of Sept 1, 2025, through the end of 2026. Sources: Congressional Research Service via EveryCRSReport, https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R48982.html ; KFF Health News, https://kffhealthnews.org/morning-breakout/health-insurers-will-cover-all-vaccines-through-2026-with-no-cost-sharing/ . The app should not promise "$0 vaccines" without a "check with your plan" note.
- **Carrier extras (examples, not guarantees):**
  - Aetna and UnitedHealthcare both offer free maternity support through Maven (an app with coaches). UHC's flyer says members can order an electric pump from an in-network supplier up to 30 days before delivery or 365 days after. Sources: Prestige PEO / Maven flyer, https://prestigepeo.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Maven-Maternity-OnePageFlyer-002.pdf ; Nova Southeastern University benefits flyer, https://nova.edu/hr/benefits/healthcare/documents/2025-free-maternity-care-program.pdf
  - Blue Cross Blue Shield is 30+ separate companies; e.g., BCBS of Michigan offers Maven. Source: https://www.bcbsm.com/amslibs/content/dam/public/employers/documents/share-resources-employees/individual-files/maternity-program-flyer-fi.pdf . I found nothing Massachusetts-specific for BCBS of MA in this pass.
  - These programs usually depend on what the employer bought, not just the carrier name.

## 4. Child Tax Credit (2026) and Massachusetts paid family leave

**Bottom line:** The 2026 Child Tax Credit is **$2,200 per child**, up to **$1,700 of it refundable**. The child and (new from 2025) at least one parent need a Social Security number. Massachusetts paid leave pays up to **$1,230.39 a week in 2026**, with **12 weeks** of bonding leave per parent in the first year, inside a 26-week yearly total.

**Facts**
- IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32, section 4.05: for 2026, maximum Child Tax Credit **$2,200**; refundable amount **$1,700**. Source: https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf (read directly). For context, the 2025 amounts were also $2,200/$1,700: IRS Tax Tip 2026-17 (Mar 3, 2026), https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/tax-benefits-for-parents-and-families
- The refundable part is 15% of earned income above $2,500; it phases out above $200,000 AGI ($400,000 married filing jointly). Source: IRS Child Tax Credit page, https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-tax-credit ; IRS Tax Tip above.
- New SSN rule: the child and at least one parent must have a valid Social Security number. Source: Kiplinger, https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/child-tax-credit (news site, not IRS; confirm on the IRS page before showing it in the app).
- Same IRS document: 2026 adoption credit up to $17,670. Source: Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.04.
- **MA PFML 2026:** maximum weekly benefit **$1,230.39** (was $1,170.64 in 2025); it equals 64% of the state's average weekly wage. Sources: Seyfarth Shaw (law firm), https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/2026-massachusetts-paid-family-and-medical-leave-updates-benefit-amount-contribution-rates-and-taxability-of-benefit-payments.html ; NFP, https://www.nfp.com/insights/2026-massachusetts-pfml-rates-and-benefits-released/ (mass.gov blocked my direct read today).
- **Weeks:** up to 12 weeks of family leave (bonding), up to 20 weeks of medical leave (e.g., recovering from birth), **26 weeks combined** per benefit year. Each parent can take their own 12 bonding weeks, within the first 12 months after birth. Sources: mass.gov, https://www.mass.gov/info-details/pfml-transitioning-from-medical-leave-to-family-leave-to-bond-with-a-child ; Brandeis HR FAQ, https://www.brandeis.edu/human-resources/employee-benefits/mapfml/faq.html ; College of the Holy Cross policy, https://holycross.edu/media/225731/download

## 5. Recall data: CPSC, FDA and NHTSA

**Bottom line:** There is a free, no-key CPSC recall API. I downloaded the whole thing today: **10,058 CPSC recall records** (1973 to Oct 22, 2026), of which about **1,600 mention baby or child gear**. FDA (openFDA) and NHTSA (car seats) also have free APIs. **"14,200 CPSC & FDA recall records" does not match any real count.** Either say "10,058 CPSC recalls" or, if adding FDA, give the real combined figure with what it means (see below).

**Facts (all checked by me on Oct 25, 2026 by calling the APIs)**
- **CPSC Recalls API:** `https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json` ; filters include `ProductName`, `Title`, `RecallDescription`, and recall dates. No key or sign-up mentioned. Source: CPSC, https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/CPSC-Recalls-Application-Program-Interface-API-Information
  - Full download (`RecallDateStart=1970-01-01`): **10,058 records**, oldest 1973-06-08, newest 2026-10-22; about 490 in 2026 so far.
  - A keyword filter (crib, stroller, infant, baby, car seat, high chair, pacifier, bassinet, etc.) matched **1,623** records; **510** since 2016. This is my rough filter, not an official CPSC category.
- **FDA (openFDA enforcement reports, free, key optional):** totals today are **29,505 food**, **18,043 drug**, **40,300 medical-device** recall entries. These count individual products in a recall, so one recall can appear many times. Baby-related food entries: **182** mention "baby," **42** "infant formula." Source: https://api.fda.gov/food/enforcement.json?limit=1 (and /drug/, /device/); docs https://open.fda.gov/apis/
- **NHTSA (car seats):** the free API `https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24C002` returns car-seat recalls (e.g., Nuna RAVA, about 608,786 seats). The "recalls by vehicle" lookup is for cars, not seats. NHTSA's bulk file (`FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip`, https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/ffdd/rcl/FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip) lists **69 child-seat recall campaigns since 2010** (campaign IDs with a "C"). Dataset listing: https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/nhtsas-office-of-defects-investigation-odi-recalls-nhtsa-api
- **Suggested honest wording:** "Checked against 10,058 CPSC recalls, 69 NHTSA car-seat recalls and FDA baby-food recalls." Have the app compute the number when it loads the data, so it is never typed in by hand (the project rules forbid made-up outputs).

## 7. Extra checks (added Oct 25, 2026)

A "529 plan" is a state-run savings account for a child's education; many states give a state tax break for putting money in. A "deduction" lowers the income you're taxed on; a "credit" comes straight off the tax bill.

**a) Massachusetts 529 (U.Fund): CONFIRMED, $1,000 single / $2,000 married filing jointly.** It's a deduction, claimed on Schedule Y, and it's per tax filer, not per child. Source: MEFA (the state agency that runs U.Fund), https://www.mefa.org/ways-to-save/mefa-u-fund/ and https://www.mefa.org/article/the-massachusetts-college-savings-tax-deduction (checked Oct 25, 2026). I did not find a separate 2026 Department of Revenue notice; MEFA does not say the amount changes by year.

**b) Indiana 529: CONFIRMED, it's a CREDIT of 20% of contributions, up to $1,500, and the cap is the SAME for single and joint filers (it does not double).** Married filing separately is $750. That has been the rule since tax year 2023. Sources: Indiana Department of Revenue Bulletin 98, https://secure.in.gov/dor/files/reference/ib98.pdf ; TaxSlayer guide, https://support.taxslayer.com/hc/en-us/articles/360049801452-How-do-I-Report-my-529-Plan-for-Indiana (checked Oct 25, 2026). I did not find a 2026-specific Indiana form.
**Oklahoma 529: CONFIRMED, a deduction of up to $10,000 single / $20,000 joint**, and contributions above the cap can be carried forward up to 5 years. Sources: 1040.com knowledge base, https://kb.1040.com/?p=4504 ; TaxSlayer Pro (2024 tax year), https://support.taxslayerpro.com/hc/en-us/articles/5780052158618-ProWeb-Where-Do-I-Enter-Contributions-to-Oklahoma-529-Plan ; Savingforcollege, https://www.savingforcollege.com/529-plans/oklahoma/oklahoma-529 (checked Oct 25, 2026). These are tax-software and industry sites; I did not find the Oklahoma Tax Commission's own 2026 page.

**c) W-4: CONFIRMED, "allowances" were removed in the 2020 redesign.** The IRS says "Allowances are no longer used for the redesigned Form W-4" (IRS, "FAQs on the 2020 Form W-4," https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/faqs-on-the-2020-form-w-4 , checked Oct 25, 2026). **"W-4 Allowance Advisor" is outdated wording**; use something like "W-4 paycheck check" or "Update your W-4 for baby." On the 2026 W-4, **Step 3 (Claim dependents and other credits)** applies if your income is $200,000 or less ($400,000 married filing jointly). Line 3(a): number of qualifying children under 17 × **$2,200**. Line 3(b): other dependents × $500. Sources: American Payroll Association, Dec 11, 2025, https://payroll.org/news-resources/news/news-detail/2025/12/11/irs-releases-2026-forms-w-4-w-4p-with-changes-due-to-obbba ; copy of the 2026 Form W-4, https://wcc.yccd.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Form-W-4.pdf (the official form is at https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw4.pdf).

**d) Car seats:**
- **Car seat recalls are NHTSA's job, not CPSC's.** Every car seat sold in the U.S. must meet NHTSA's federal standard (FMVSS 213), and recalls run under NHTSA rules. A stricter version (FMVSS 213b) becomes mandatory Dec 5, 2026. Sources: NHTSA, https://www.nhtsa.gov/node/51746 ; NHTSA recall letter (26C005, 2026), https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/rcl/2026/RCAK-26C005-2704.pdf ; National Law Review, https://natlawreview.com/node/267417/printable/print . CPSC covers other baby gear such as strollers, cribs and stand-alone carriers. That split is general knowledge; I did not find an official statement of it today.
- **There is NO make/model car seat lookup in NHTSA's free API.** I tested it today: `recallsByVehicle?make=GRACO&model=SNUGRIDE 35` returns 0 results for every model year tried, including 9999, the year the bulk file uses for seats. What does work for free with no key:
  1. **Recall-number lookup:** `https://api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber?campaignNumber=24C002` (returns a Nuna RAVA car seat recall).
  2. **Bulk file**, refreshed daily: https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/ffdd/rcl/FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip . Car seat rows have a "C" in the recall number and list brand and model names. It has 69 car-seat recalls since 2010, including Graco SNUGRIDE / SNUGRIDE 35 (14C004) and SNUGRIDE TURN & SLIDE (26C003).
  For the app: load the bulk file once, keep the "C" rows, and match on brand plus model name.
- **Graco SnugRide 35 Lite is a real product line** (an infant car seat for 4-35 lb, including the "LX" version). Sources: Graco Canada, https://www.gracobaby.ca/en_CA/Infant%20Car%20Seats/SAP_2143034.html ; Kohl's, https://www.kohls.com/product/prd-4243645/graco-snugride-35-lite-lx-infant-car-seat.jsp . **I found no listing for model number 2120349.** Graco's own item numbers for this line look like 2143034, 2161295 and 2119658, so 2120349 is plausible-looking but not confirmed.
