Do these five things first

Biggest & easiest to miss

Claim the Trump Account

The government set up an account for your baby on Oct 1, but the $1,000 only arrives if you claim it and opt in. Do it as soon as the baby's Social Security number arrives.

Already have paid maternity leave from work?

Short answer: yes, she can still get state paid leave (PFML). Massachusetts lets the two work together. The catch: her total weekly pay can't go above her normal pay, and the weeks usually run at the same time, not back to back. How it plays out depends on which of three setups her employer uses.

Most common

Work pays maternity leave or disability

She still applies to the state. The state only cuts its check if her combined pay from both would go over her normal weekly pay. Many employers "coordinate": the state pays its part, and the employer pays the difference so she gets 100%. The employer's leave usually counts toward the same 26-week limit.

mass.gov: how other leave affects PFML · what to report (PDF)
Some large employers

Work has its own "private plan"

Some employers get state approval to run their own leave plan instead of the state's. If so, she applies through her employer or its insurance company, not the state. A state application would be denied. The plan must be at least as generous as the state's.

mass.gov: private plan rules · exemption process
Vacation & sick days

"Topping up" with PTO

Since November 2023 she can use vacation or sick days on top of state leave pay, up to 100% of her normal pay. It's her choice; her employer can't force it, but its PTO rules decide which kinds of time count. She can also use PTO to get paid during the unpaid first week.

mass.gov: topping off FAQ · employee FAQ

What it means for the ~$17,350 estimate: If work already pays her full salary for 12 weeks, PFML mostly changes who pays for those weeks; it doesn't add money. The new money comes from weeks work doesn't cover. Example: work pays 100% for 12 weeks and she takes 20 weeks in total. Weeks 13–20 are paid only by the state: about 8 × $913 ≈ $7,300 she wouldn't get otherwise.

Four questions to ask HR:

  1. Do we use the state PFML program or our own private plan?
  2. Does our maternity leave pay the difference on top of PFML, or does it get reduced by it?
  3. Does our leave run at the same time as PFML, or after it?
  4. Which kinds of PTO can I use to top up?

Every benefit we found

The labels show how sure we are. Confirmed means we checked an official page that's live in 2026. Partly confirmed means the program exists, but some details are dated. Unconfirmed means check before relying on it. Income limit marks programs that only lower-income families can use.

Federal

.gov programs & tax breaks

Trump Account ($1,000 Treasury deposit)

A government investment account for every US-citizen baby born 2025–2028, with a one-time $1,000 deposit. The money is locked until the child turns 18. Family can add up to $5,000 a year, and employers can add up to $2,500 of that tax-free.

Who: US citizen child with an SSN. Action: Accounts were auto-created Oct 1, 2026, but you must claim it and opt in to the $1,000 through the Trump Accounts app or IRS Form 4547. Note: Dell's $250 gift covers kids born 2016–2024, not 2026 babies.

irs.gov/trumpaccounts · Form 4547
$1,000Partly confirmed

Child Tax Credit

Takes up to $2,200 per child off your federal taxes. Up to $1,700 of it is paid to you even if you owe no tax.

Who: Child under 17 with an SSN, and the parent needs a work-eligible SSN. Shrinks above $400k income (married). Action: Claim on your 2026 tax return (Schedule 8812), due April 15, 2027.

IRS Child Tax Credit · Rev. Proc. 2025-32
$2,200Confirmed

Dependent Care FSA (pre-tax daycare account)

An account through work where you pay daycare costs with money taxes haven't touched. The limit rose to $7,500 per household in 2026.

Who: Only if your employer offers it. Action: Enroll within ~30 days of birth or at open enrollment. Est.: $7,500 × ~35% combined federal, payroll, and state tax ≈ $2,600; we count $2,400 to be safe. You can't use the same dollars for the childcare tax credit too.

IRS childcare credit info
~$2,400Confirmed

Free breast pump & breastfeeding help (Affordable Care Act)

Most health plans must cover a breast pump and breastfeeding counseling with no copay. HSA/FSA health accounts can also pay for extra pump supplies.

Who: Nearly everyone with insurance; older "grandfathered" plans are the exception. Action: Order through an in-network supplier such as Aeroflow.

healthcare.gov · IRS Pub 502 · Babylist: free pump guide & eligibility check
~$200Confirmed

Child & Dependent Care Credit

A tax credit for part of your daycare costs, worth up to $1,500 for one child in 2026 (it was more generous before 2026). Use this or the FSA, whichever saves more. For most two-income families, that's the FSA.

Action: Form 2441 on your tax return. Keep daycare receipts and the provider's tax ID.

529 college account → Roth IRA rollover

A 529 is a college savings account where growth is tax-free. Open it at birth: after 15 years, up to $35,000 of unused money can move into your child's own Roth IRA (a tax-free retirement account).

Action: Open the Massachusetts U.Fund (this also unlocks the $50 BabySteps deposit and a state tax deduction).

Savingforcollege.com
Long-termPartly confirmed

FMLA job protection

A federal law that protects your job and health insurance for up to 12 weeks off. It's unpaid; the Massachusetts PFML program is what pays you.

Who: Employers with 50+ workers, and you've worked there 12+ months.

Massachusetts

mass.gov programs

Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML)

The state pays you while you're out: up to 20 weeks to recover from birth and 12 weeks to bond, 26 weeks combined. The 2026 maximum is $1,230/week. Your partner can take their own 12 bonding weeks too.

Est.: On a $65k salary, ≈ $913/week × 19 paid weeks. Action: Apply at paidleave.mass.gov, up to 60 days before leave or within 90 days after it starts. Bonding leave must end before the baby's 1st birthday. Paid leave from work too? How the two combine.

If your income qualifies

These don't apply to the $130k example family, but they're often the biggest money for families earning less. For example, a family of 3 earning $45,000 could qualify for most of them.

WIC (free formula & food)

Free formula (up to ~9–10 cans a month), healthy food for mom, breastfeeding help, and pumps. Income under 185% of the poverty line ($50,542 for a family of 3). You qualify automatically if you're on MassHealth or SNAP.

Action: mass.gov/forms/apply-for-wic-online or 1-800-942-1007.

MassHealth (free health insurance)

Covers mom through pregnancy and 12 months after birth, and the baby automatically for a year. Income limit: 200% of the poverty line for pregnancy coverage, up to 300% for kids.

Action: mahealthconnector.org or 800-841-2900.

Earned Income Tax Credit (federal + state)

A tax refund for working families: up to $4,427 federal with one child, plus 40% more from Massachusetts (up to ~$1,771). Example: a married couple earning $45k gets about $2,215 federal + $886 state.

How we estimated

The family: Married, filing taxes jointly, about $130,000 combined income. Mom earns about $65,000 at a Massachusetts employer with health insurance through work. Baby born October 2026, partly formula-fed, starting daycare in 2027.

Cash and tax items use the official 2026 amounts (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Paid leave uses the 2026 state formula: 80% of pay up to $961/week, plus 50% above that (mass.gov, Foley, Jan 2026). The FSA assumes ~35% combined federal, payroll, and state tax on $7,500. First-year cost of ~$20,400 is from a BabyCenter survey; Massachusetts is the priciest state to raise a child (SmartAsset via Credit Karma). Store deals use the low-to-middle end of each range, assuming a typical family uses some but not all of them. Free services are valued at roughly what the private version costs.

Not counted: small freebies like sample boxes, diaper samples, and product testing (listed, but marked "Not in total"), income-limited programs, your partner's paid leave, and anything you'd only use if a problem comes up (Early Intervention, donor milk).

Caveats: The Trump Account's final rules aren't out yet, and we couldn't independently confirm that the $1,000 deposits are arriving; only the White House has said so. A few store offers (Amazon diaper discounts, Nordstrom registry perks) couldn't be confirmed, so they're left out of the total. This report isn't tax or financial advice; check the specifics with a tax preparer.
